Iowa Liquor Sales Analysis

What sells, where, and when across 2.92 million Iowa liquor records from 2025? I answered that question entirely in PostgreSQL, and the most important finding was not a bestseller. It was 429,996 repeated rows quietly inflating revenue by $76.75 million.

01 · the data

2,920,615 rows, 16 columns, one year of liquor sales.

The data comes from the Iowa government data portal and was loaded into PostgreSQL as public.iowa_food_establishments. It covers January to December 2025 with order dates, store details, county information, product descriptions, vendors, bottle costs and retail prices, quantities, and recorded sales values. The main measures are sales_dollars, sales_bottles, sales_liters, and sales_gallons.

How it was queried

All analysis ran as SQL against the database. Aggregate functions (SUM(), COUNT(), AVG(), MIN(), MAX()) produced the headline metrics. GROUP BY compared revenue across months, categories, products, counties, and stores. Date functions (DATE_TRUNC(), EXTRACT(), TO_CHAR()) built the monthly and day-of-week views. The LAG() window function compared each category against its previous month. And SELECT DISTINCT powered the duplicate investigation. The original table was never altered.

02 · the year in months

Eleven steady months, then September doubles everything.

Recorded revenue rose gradually from $31.11M in January to $37.65M in August. Then September posted $70.32M and October $78.19M, roughly double the surrounding months, before November fell back to $34.68M. Plotted against deduplicated totals, the story sharpens: January through July match exactly, while September and October collapse back toward $35M and $40M. Those two spikes were repeated records, not a sales boom.

03 · what sells

Three Titos sizes take three of the top four spots.

American vodkas lead categories at $76.23M, followed by Canadian whiskies at $54.25M and straight bourbon at $47.29M. Whiskey in its forms holds four of the top seven spots. At product level, three sizes of Titos Handmade Vodka take three of the top four positions, totaling about $32M between them, with Black Velvet and Captain Morgan close behind.

Revenue per bottle tells a different story

Ranking by revenue per bottle surfaces the luxury end: rare whiskies and cognacs at thousands of dollars a bottle, sold in tiny quantities. A Weller Millennium at $5,625 a bottle across 22 bottles and a single-bottle Macallan M at $5,449 show the opposite business from Titos. Revenue per bottle describes the mix, not a shelf price, since pack sizes and bottle volumes vary.

04 · where it sells

Polk County alone moves 23% of revenue.

Polk County recorded $115.89M across 310 stores, more than the next two counties combined. Linn follows at $42.81M. Revenue per store adds context, since a county with more stores naturally totals more. Store-level leaders include Hy-Vee #3 in Des Moines at $16.22M and Central City 2 at $15.60M. High recorded revenue alone does not prove efficiency, since costs, store size, and foot traffic sit outside this data.

05 · when orders land

Distributors order on weekdays. Weekends go quiet.

Tuesday and Wednesday each recorded over $106M, with Monday close at $98.39M. Thursday and Friday sit lower at $88.83M and $94.40M. Then activity collapses: Sunday recorded $6.86M across 49,640 records, and Saturday just $154K across 1,285 records, against more than 600,000 records on a busy Tuesday. The ordered_on field marks the order date, not the consumer purchase date, so this reads as distributor and retailer scheduling, not drinking habits.

06 · the repeated records

429,996 repeated rows holding $76,750,656.13.

The original table holds 2,920,615 rows. Selecting distinct full rows returns 2,490,619, a difference of 429,996 repeated rows. Revenue drops from $501,564,942.14 to $424,814,286.01, a gap of $76,750,656.13. Bottle volume drops from 35,894,220 to 30,440,723, a gap of 5,453,497. One inspected combination of date, store, item, vendor, quantity, and value appeared 28 times with identical values.

A finding, not a verdictRepeated rows are not automatically errors. Legitimate transactions can share identical values across every column when the table has no unique transaction identifier. The comparison shows the size of the uncertainty, and the monthly table above shows where it concentrates, before anyone uses these months to claim business growth.
07 · what is missing

1,213,531 rows with no cost. No margin math possible.

State bottle cost and retail price are each missing in 1,213,531 rows, about 41.6%. Without them, no complete profit-margin calculation is possible. County and city names are each missing in 1,931 rows, a small share that can still shift local summaries. A handful of other columns miss 2 values each. Missing values were left as missing: filling them with zero would invent a price of $0.00, which is a claim, not a fix.

the bottom line

Quote the total only after checking it.

PostgreSQL turned 2,920,615 Iowa liquor records into a full business picture: $501.56M recorded revenue, American vodkas on top, Polk County far ahead, ordering concentrated on weekdays, and luxury bottles fetching thousands per unit. Then validation changed the picture: 429,996 repeated rows inflate the total by $76.75M, concentrated in August through October, which means the September and October spikes cannot be read as growth without source checks.

Together with 41.6% missing cost values, one year of coverage, and no customer or inventory data, the limits are clear. The next stage moves to Python for visualization and demand analysis. The lesson stands: useful analysis requires examining the data before interpreting the numbers as business performance.

Totals describe the table. Validation decides whether to trust them.
