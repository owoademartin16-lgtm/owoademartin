Retail Performance Analysis & Sales Strategy

Eleven months of till receipts: 125,751 transactions, June 2025 through April 2026. I read them the way an owner would. Which products make the money? When do customers buy most? And can tomorrow's sales be predicted? The receipts answered all three.

01 · what I started with

125,751 receipts, zero excuses.

The raw file held 125,751 rows and 17 columns: dates, sales types, return flags, suppliers, product codes, divisions, stores, channels, quantities, sales amounts, and cost of goods. First I cleaned the data. The Reason of Return column was empty in every single row, all 125,751 of them, so it was removed. No duplicate rows anywhere. That left 16 usable columns running June 1, 2025 to April 24, 2026.

Here is the business at a glance: 2,326 products across 40 stores, moving 116,995 units. That is roughly 386 receipts a day at about $83 each. And here is the number that stands out: 74.7% of everything sold at full price. Promos took 18.8%, markdowns and clearance the rest. When three out of four customers pay full ticket, pricing is working.

02 · where the money comes from

A few products drive most revenue.

The ten biggest categories pull in $9,517,939.45, about 91% of total net revenue. Nearly the whole shop fits in one chart. Step back and the full business splits three ways: Scholar Footwear at $7.34M (70.0%), Femme Footwear at $2.71M (25.9%), and Junior Apparel at $0.43M (4.1%). Scholar brings in most of the revenue.

What the mix is saying

Two products drive the business. Retro Legend and Trailblazer in the Scholar line combine for $3.83M, or 40.2% of category sales. Two out of every five dollars comes from these two products.

Trailblazer sells across both lines. Same shoe, two customer groups: $1.91M in Scholar plus $1.24M in Femme, more than $3.15M combined. This makes Trailblazer a flagship product.

Femme depends too much on one product. Scholar spreads its revenue across six collections. Femme gets roughly half its line revenue from Trailblazer alone, while Flight Classic and Boot Collection sit near $300K each. This is an opportunity to grow a second strong product in the Femme line.

03 · what the business keeps

Profit matters more than revenue.

Revenue shows what customers paid. Profit shows what the business kept. Every receipt in this data carries its cost of goods, so each dollar was followed down to profit. Costs totaled $5,847,751.10, leaving $4,634,359.15 in gross profit. That is a 44.21% margin: of every $100 a customer hands over, about $44 stays with the business.

Now look at profit by category and the ranking changes. Trailblazer in Scholar made $927,715, the highest profit of any category, even though Retro Legend sold more dollars. Scholar Boot Collection is also worth attention: seventh by revenue but fourth by profit at $358K, because it keeps 54.3 cents of every dollar.

One concern: Femme Trailblazer keeps only 35.2 cents per dollar, the lowest margin among the leading products. The line's best-selling product earns the least per sale. If Femme grows, it must grow profitably, which means building a second strong product beside Trailblazer.

04 · the money that came back

About 6% of receipts are returns.

Returns deserve close attention. 7,491 receipts out of 125,751, about 6.0%, were returned. They removed $733,571 from sales and $325,795 from profit. That is a direct loss: roughly seven percent of everything the business kept.

A clear pattern: the most returned products are the best sellers. Scholar Trailblazer returns totaled -$122,146, Femme Trailblazer -$115,073, Retro Legend -$103,270. This is mostly driven by sales volume: the products that sell the most are returned the most. Still, Trailblazer ranks first in both sales and returns, so better fit and sizing advice for that product could reduce losses.

Reading the waves

August lines up with back to school demand. Revenue of $1.54M represents a 79.4% increase over July. School shoe buying is a plausible driver, though the receipts alone cannot prove it.

December lines up with the holiday season. Revenue of $1.79M was the highest month in the data, up 104.8% on November. This is more than double a normal month.

January fell hard after the holidays. With only one January in the data, this is a single observation, not proof of a yearly pattern. Sales fell to $565K once holiday spending ended. February and March recovered to $1.04M and $934K. Then April slid to $507K, the weakest of all eleven months. One weak month can be normal variation, but two in a row would signal a trend, so April deserves close monitoring.

06 · inside the biggest month

December has its own bestseller list.

December's $1.79M follows a different ranking from the full year. Retro Legend still led at $330,846. But third place went to Scholar Boot Collection at $219,919, up from seventh overall. Cold weather increased boot demand sharply. Femme Boot Collection shows this even more clearly: $134,576 in December alone, about 44% of its entire eleven-month total. If boot stock runs low in November, the business loses December revenue.

07 · where it sold

One store leads by 21%.

Forty stores share the $10.48M, but the distribution is uneven. STR-1006 recorded $485,561, a full 21% above the next store. It also leads on profit at $225,775 with a 46.5% margin. Behind it, nine stores fall between $328K and $400K. The key question is what STR-1006 does differently: location, customer traffic, staffing, or product range. Identifying the cause means it can be applied across the other 39 stores.

08 · predicting tomorrow

From receipts to a forecast.

Analysis explains the past. Forecasting predicts the future. I combined all 125,751 receipts into 326 daily totals, then extended each day with past sales figures: yesterday, last week, two weeks back, a month back, plus 7-day and 30-day averages, calendar flags, and a running day count. The first 30 days were needed to calculate these history features, leaving 296 days for modeling. The earliest 236 trained the models and the latest 60 tested them, in strict calendar order, so no model was trained on future data.

Three models were tested. Random Forest reached R² 0.767 with an average error of $5,996 per day. Gradient Boosting reached 0.740. CatBoost won clearly at R² 0.865, with an average error of only $4,324 per day. That model was saved as Business Sales.joblib and now powers the live application. To confirm: yes, the model predicts one number, the next day's total sales revenue in dollars.

A further test was run to improve CatBoost with a 15-round search over its settings. The best combination found scored R² 0.819, below the untuned 0.865. Adjusting settings on a few hundred days of data can make results worse rather than better, so the untuned model was kept and the tuned version was not used.

The test forecasts show reasonable errors: a few thousand dollars in either direction on $25K to $99K days, with an occasional $17K difference on a volatile day.

09 · what to do about it

Prepare stock before peak periods.

Inventory and replenishment

Order extra Retro Legend and Trailblazer stock in late June, well before the August sales increase. Set reorder points (ROP) that account for the August and December peaks instead of reacting after they start. Those two months alone move over $3.3M. Running out of stock there costs more than anywhere else in the year. Also increase boot orders by November: December results show boots sell strongly in winter.

Grow the Femme line, profitably

Apply the marketing approach that made Femme Trailblazer successful to Flight Classic and Boot Collection. Combine them with high-volume Scholar lines in cross-promotions to increase basket size. But watch the margin: Femme Trailblazer keeps only 35 cents per dollar, so growth should come from controlled spending, not broad discounts.

Protect the price, chase the returns

Three quarters of everything sells at full price. Protect that with limited discounting. The larger concern is returns: 6% of receipts remove $326K of profit, led by the Trailblazer products. Better fit and sizing advice on those product pages would recover part of this loss.

Copy STR-1006

Review how STR-1006 operates and apply the findings to the other 39 stores. A 21% lead is either a practice worth copying or a data issue worth understanding. Both outcomes are valuable.

the bottom line

Concentration plus seasonality sets the strategy.

Two Scholar products drive 40% of category sales and keep nearly half of every dollar. One product carries Femme at a lower margin. Two months produce far more revenue than the rest. Returns remove 7% of profit. And a CatBoost model predicting daily sales at R² 0.865 turns that history into one number to plan each day around.

So here is the recommended plan: stock Retro Legend and Trailblazer before each peak, build Femme a second strong product, reduce Trailblazer returns with better fit guidance, and apply what STR-1006 does across all stores.

Know which products earn the most, know when demand peaks, and stock up before it does.
