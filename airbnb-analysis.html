Amsterdam Airbnb Nightly Price Prediction

What should an Amsterdam Airbnb cost per night? I worked with 10,369 listings from the Inside Airbnb Amsterdam data set (June 2026) to find out what drives the price, then built a model that predicts the nightly rate from the listing itself. It lands within about €83 of the real price.

01 · the data

10,369 Amsterdam listings in one June 2026 snapshot.

Inside Airbnb publishes full listing scrapes for Amsterdam. The June 15, 2026 file holds 10,369 listings across 90 columns: host details, location, room and bed counts, availability calendars, review scores, amenities, and nightly prices. Each row is one specific place a guest could book, from a €52 shared room to an €11,412 canal mansion.

Not every listing carries a price. Only 6,377 listings had a usable nightly rate, and those became the working data set. After final outlier filtering, about 6,370 listings went into modeling. That filtering is not a weakness in the work. A listing without a price cannot teach pricing. A duplicate check found zero duplicate rows.

02 · cleaning

13 columns held nothing. 5 more only identified rows.

Thirteen columns were missing in every single row, including host response time, calendar status, and neighbourhood group fields, so all thirteen were dropped. Identifier and link columns (listing id, URLs, scrape ids, picture links) describe where a row came from, not what it costs, and were removed with the long free-text fields. That cut the table from 90 columns to 71.

Then came the price column itself. Missing in 3,992 rows (38.5%), it decided the working set: listings without a price cannot train a pricing model. Columns with moderate gaps stayed and were handled during modeling: bathrooms (16.7% missing among priced listings), bedrooms (14.3%), beds (12.0%), and review scores (about 10%, mostly listings too new to have reviews yet). Hosts skip boring fields and new listings have no history. Both patterns are normal, and the modeling accounts for them instead of deleting the rows.

03 · what the market looks like

8,489 entire homes against 1,833 private rooms.

Amsterdam rents out whole apartments first and spare rooms second. Entire homes make up 81.9% (8,489 listings) and private rooms 17.7% (1,833). Hotel rooms (26) and shared rooms (21) barely register. The most common property setup is an entire rental unit (4,451 listings), and the most common booking sleeps 2 guests (5,412 listings), then 4 guests (3,407). Entire homes also charge the most at a €331 median, nearly double the €172 for private rooms, with shared rooms at €52.

Room type is not property type

Two columns sound alike but measure different things. Room type is the booking category with 4 values: an entire place, a private room, a hotel room, or a shared room. Property type describes the building itself across 59 values, such as rental unit, condo, townhouse, or houseboat. Your GitHub chart shows property types, while the chart above shows room types. Both views matter, so here is the property side: entire rental units lead by far at 4,451 listings.

Prices run €15 to over €11,000

Among priced listings, the median night costs €291 and the mean €347. The gap between those two numbers already hints at the shape: a long tail of luxury listings pulls the average up. The most common price band is €300 to €400 (1,356 listings), while 107 listings sit above €1,000 a night, topping out past €11,000. So a model that only learns the middle would fail both a budget room and a canal mansion. It has to cover the full stretch.

Location moves the median from €120 to €336

Across 22 neighbourhoods, the pattern is strong. De Pijp leads at a €335.56 median, with Zuid (€334.00) and De Baarsjes (€330.49) close behind. At the other end, Bijlmer-Oost sits at €120.30, with Osdorp (€152.75) and Bijlmer-Centrum (€160.00) nearby. The most expensive district charges nearly triple the cheapest. Where a listing sits is one of the strongest price signals in the data, which is why the model gets a per-neighbourhood price feature of its own.

The rest of the picture

The typical listing has 1 bathroom, 1 bedroom, and 1 bed, requires a 2-night minimum stay, and holds a median of 10 reviews. About 10% of listings have no reviews at all, which usually means they are new, and those listings are the hardest to price because past guests have said nothing yet. Only 16.8% of hosts are Superhosts, so the badge still means something. And the median listing is free just 25 days a year: Amsterdam books up, which is exactly why getting the nightly number right matters.

04 · preparing the target

From skew 16.69 to a scale models can learn.

Raw prices skew hard: skew score 16.69, with a maximum above €11,000 against a €291 median. Models learn badly from that shape because a handful of extreme listings pull every average off center, so the priciest outliers were filtered first, bringing skew down to 3.28. Both log and square root transforms were tested to straighten what was left, and the numbers came out at -0.11 for log against 1.22 for square root. Modeling ran on the square root of price, and predictions were squared back into real euros at the end, which is why the final error reads directly in euros per night.

05 · what the model sees

24 columns stand in for each apartment.

A guest judges a listing by photos and gut feeling. A model needs columns, so each apartment became 24 of them, grouped the way a host would think. The host: Superhost status, listing count, verified identity. The place: latitude, longitude, plus a smoothed average price for its neighbourhood, so small areas do not swing on one listing and unseen areas fall back to the city average. The space: guests, bathrooms, bedrooms, beds. The rules: minimum and maximum nights, open days a year. The reputation: review count, rating, reviews per month. The extras: amenity count, with room type as separate yes/no flags.

06 · the models

CatBoost 0.7037, LightGBM 0.7034, Gradient Boosting 0.7023.

Four approaches trained on 80% of listings and faced the unseen 20%. Linear Regression managed R² 0.5813, respectable for a straight line through 24 columns, but far behind the trees. The three tree boosters landed within two thousandths of each other, and CatBoost finished first at R² 0.7037 with an average miss of €83.32 per night. That model was saved for the app, with its feature order stored alongside it.

Look at training scores next to test scores and a second story appears. LightGBM memorized the training data best at 0.8545 but kept only 0.7034 on new listings. CatBoost memorized less (0.7727) and kept the most (0.7038). The model that learned the least by heart generalized the best, which is exactly the trade-off to watch.

07 · from notebook to app

The saved model behind the price box.

The saved package holds three things: the trained CatBoost model, the neighbourhood price lookup, and the city average fallback for areas the model has never seen. A sample check ran a 2-guest listing in Bijlmer-Centrum with solid reviews through the full pipeline and returned about €258 a night. That sits below the €291 city median, exactly where a cheaper district should land, so the model behaves sensibly where it can be checked. The Streamlit app wraps the same pipeline: enter the listing details and receive an instant nightly estimate.

What a host can take from this

Price against your own district median first, not the city average. A De Pijp host and a Bijlmer host play different games. An entire home earns roughly double a private room, so space pays. Reviews move bookings, and one in ten listings has none yet, which means new hosts price in the dark until the first guests report back. And with the typical calendar open only 25 days a year, every mispriced week costs real occupancy.

the bottom line

The price of a night, explained by the data.

Amsterdam nightly prices center near €291 with a long luxury tail past €11,000. Entire homes dominate the market at nearly double the private room rate. Location moves the median from €120 in Bijlmer-Oost to €336 in De Pijp. A CatBoost model using 24 listing facts predicts new nightly rates at R² 0.7037 with an €83.32 average miss, and it generalizes better than rivals that memorized more.

Know what each listing offers, know where it sits, and price it against the neighborhood.
